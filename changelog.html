Changelog

2026-09-29: Site reformatting + bugfixes

A round of layout, navigation, and browser changes.

  • Pages and navigation: merged the Downloads and Citation pages into a single Download and Cite page (downloads and schema first, then citation guidance), and added Changelog to the top navigation. The top menu now reads Browse, Statistics, Methods, Download and Cite, About, Changelog.
  • Browse table: filters are now multi-select checkbox dropdowns (options within a filter are combined with OR, separate filters with AND). The 25-per-page pager is replaced by a single scrollable table with pinned column headers, sized to fit the page width. The Phenotype column is renamed "Observed phenotype", matching the variant detail panel.
  • Citation and attribution: added Devin Friedrich, Suriyen Subramaniam, Vedaamrutha Reddy, and William Yang to the database citation. Acknowledgments now lists authors and data compilation/curation contributors separately.
  • Layout and text: trimmed redundant intro sentences and callout boxes sections. Reformatted the mobile site for accessibility.
  • Bug fixes: links to a section of a page (e.g. "see the scope", "Browse the variants") no longer land with the heading hidden under the sticky top bar; the Statistics page's "Methods" links now open the Validation tier legend and Base-editing feasibility sections directly instead of the top of Methods; the browse table no longer scrolls sideways on desktop because of long HGVS strings; the top navigation switches to the menu button before its tabs can overflow; filter dropdowns are no longer clipped by the browser panel; checkboxes and scrollbars now follow the manual light/dark toggle.

2026-08-14: NIH-convention & domain-accuracy audit

Ran two independent audits: a website-design pass comparing the site's text and structure against NCBI/NIH resource-page conventions (ClinVar's "What is ClinVar?", the NCBI variation guide), and a blood group genetics domain pass checking terminology and factual framing against ISBT nomenclature. Changes made as a result:

  • Data fixes: RHD-NC-001 had an internal AI-scoring/policy-review note sitting in the alternative_notation field (meant for legacy nomenclature); moved it into molecular_mechanism as a bracketed audit note.
  • New content: a "What is the Blood Group Variant Database?" opening statement and an FAQ section (About), a field-by-field data dictionary and a formal References section (Methods), and an NLM/Citing-Medicine-format citation (Citation) in place of the ad hoc "Available at... Accessed..." version.
  • Tone/formatting: converted the hero's CTA buttons and disclaimer pill badge to plain hyperlinks and a trailing sentence (NCBI resource pages use plain links, not marketing-style buttons); flattened the Methods disclaimer from a tinted callout box into a plain paragraph; fixed a "Stats"/"Statistics" nav-label mismatch and a "Validation evidence tier"/"Validation tier" naming inconsistency; added a visible data-release date to Downloads and Methods.

2026-08-13: Full citation audit + site expansion

Cross-checked the cited PMIDs against live PubMed metadata, expanding on the prior sample audit.

Also added: the Statistics, Downloads, About, and this Changelog page; a working mobile navigation menu; CSV export regenerated automatically alongside the JSON on every data change.

2026-08-12: Initial site build + sample citation audit

Rebuilt the site from the original data compilation: normalized the source tables into one shared schema, replaced the prior generic branding with proper Chai Lab / Jun Liu attribution, and designed the searchable browser, Methods, and Citation pages.

Spot-checked a sample of variants against PubMed, surfacing the need for the full audit completed the next day.

Original compilation

Non-coding and regulatory RHD variants compiled by Jun Liu, MD, PhD from primary literature, ISBT allele tables, and RhesusBase.

About this resource

A research catalog of non-coding and regulatory blood group variants, compiled for the study of variant-guided base editing strategies for antigen modulation. Not a clinical or diagnostic tool.
